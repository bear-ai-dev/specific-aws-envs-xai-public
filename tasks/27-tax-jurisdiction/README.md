## The capability, in one sentence

Deciding what tax an invoice owes — the regime each business is configured with,
the exemption a customer may hold, the rate quoted by the tax authority for the
buyer's destination against the right account, the VAT identity the two parties
present to each other, and the transaction filed back to the authority once the
invoice settles.

## What the agent gets

A NestJS billing service with that capability cut out. Invoices still assemble,
price their lines, persist, settle and generate PDFs; they simply come out with
`salesTaxRate: 0` and no tax line, nothing is ever asked of the tax authority,
and no VAT registration appears on either address block. The container serves a
sandbox estate of five businesses through a local emulator, plus a month of the
billing those businesses already ran and the tax authority's own log of every
request the platform made for them.

The recorded month is deliberately thin: nine invoices, four rate lookups and
five filed transactions, sized so that every case the grader distinguishes has
exactly one instance and no case has two. See "Sandbox floor" below.

### One thing removed that is not tax determination

`src/setting/dto/taxJarAuthorizer.ts` (134 lines) validates a submitted TaxJar
key by calling the provider. It is tax-specific and its behaviour is observable
elsewhere, but the direct reason it went is mechanical: it sat in an import cycle
between `read-setting.dto.ts` and `update-settings.dto.ts` that only became
load-bearing once `TaxModule` was gone, and it produced a temporal-dead-zone
`ReferenceError` at startup. Removing it breaks the cycle. The reference solution
does **not** restore it, and nothing grades it.

### Structurally different implementation

Shipped as `solution/alternative.patch` so the calibration is reproducible. It
scores 1.0 on all 256 checks and shares no structure with the reference:

- No NestJS service, no `@Injectable`, no module, no dependency injection. Tax
  determination is three plain exported functions in a new file,
  `src/invoice/taxDetermination.ts`: `determineRate`, `fileTransaction`,
  `vatLine`, plus a local `inEuropeanUnion` predicate.
- No `taxjar` npm client. It calls the authority with bare `fetch` and builds the
  request bodies by hand.
- No `TaxableLineItem` / `TaxableLineItems` classes; plain object literals.
- No `getSalesTaxRate` method on the invoice. The regime decision is inlined into
  `determineRate`, which the entity calls from `generate` with the fields it
  needs passed as arguments rather than read off `this`.
- The EU list is a local array literal, not the JSON import.
- Settlement calls `fileTransaction` directly from `updateStatus`; the payment
  processor is not involved and takes no tax collaborator.

That it scores 1.0 is the evidence that the verifier grades behaviour. Nothing in
`compute_reward.py` reads submitted source; it reads the records the driver
captured and the authority's log pulled over an admin channel the agent has no
token for.

## Sandbox floor

The recorded month was cut from eleven invoices to **nine**, six rate lookups to
**four**, and six filed transactions to **five**. Two rows were removed because
they were second instances of a class already present, not because the class
stopped mattering:

- `cus_broadmoor` (Oregon, zero-rate jurisdiction) repeated `cus_hartwell`'s
  class — plain `meteringcoCalculated`, sandbox, settled and filed. No held-out case
  is quoted a zero rate *by the authority*, so nothing distinguished was lost.
- `cus_bergstrom` (Berlin, `Open`) repeated `cus_dunmore` for VAT on both sides
  and `cus_ironvale` for the production account, and its `Open` status meant it
  demonstrated nothing about filing.

Everything that survives is the sole instance of at least one class the grader
distinguishes:

| row | the only sandbox instance of |
| --- | --- |
| `cus_hartwell` | `meteringcoCalculated` baseline; the full request body and filed order payload — the wire format |
| `cus_larkspur` | exemption under `meteringcoCalculated`; also that an exempt invoice still files, at zero |
| `cus_seaford` | an address the authority refuses — absorbed, invoice issues anyway |
| `cus_wexley` | settled with a state-less destination, so nothing is filed |
| `cus_ironvale` | production routing; the category multiplier and its unrounded rate; a US buyer holding a VAT identity and getting no line |
| `cus_dunmore` | the `manual` regime actually charging; VAT printed on both sides |
| `cus_fairholme` | exemption beating a manual rate |
| `cus_caldwell` | `meteringcoCalculated` with no credential — refused, with the verbatim message |
| `cus_ashgrove` | no regime despite a stored rate; and no credential outside `meteringcoCalculated` issuing normally without filing |

Two rows are load-bearing as a *pair* and were kept for that reason: the single
`biz_lumen` lookup carries `"environment": "production"` and the `biz_northwind`
lookups carry `"environment": "sandbox"`, and it takes both to pin the routing.
The recording does not show that the two authorities hold different rate tables
for the same jurisdiction, which is what the held-out `verdant` cases punish.

One gap was closed while thinning rather than opened by it. The rule in section 9
is that EU membership decides the VAT line, not holding a registration, and it
decides for each party independently. The sandbox demonstrated that only on the
buyer side, through `cus_ironvale`. `biz_northwind` now holds
`US-EIN-51-0793344` in `estate.json` and prints no VAT line in any recorded
invoice, mirroring the held-out `biz_calloway`, which holds
`US-EIN-84-2213097`. That costs no extra row.

## Difficulty

Against the "fewer than 4 solves in 8" target, my estimate is now **1–2 solves in
8**, revised down from 2–3 after thinning the recorded month.

The revision is one step, not two, and here is the reasoning. Before thinning,
the most valuable thing a solver could do was read the recording as a template:
eleven invoices covering nine classes meant two classes had a second worked
example, and more importantly a solver could triangulate. Two `meteringcoCalculated`
sandbox invoices next to each other let you check a rate hypothesis against a
second data point before writing anything. `cus_bergstrom` next to `cus_dunmore`
gave two independent confirmations of the VAT-both-sides shape. Both of those
are gone. Every remaining row now has to be read for the one thing it teaches,
and a misread of any single row costs the whole reward.

What I do *not* claim is that thinning made the rules harder to find. Nine of
nine still have a route, and section 9's route is stronger than it was. So this
is a change in how much confirmation a solver gets, not in whether the answer is
derivable — which is why I moved one step rather than claiming the floor.

What makes it hard:

- Nine independent rules must all be right at once for a binary reward, and three
  of them are precedence rules that only bite in combination: exemption over a
  manual rate, no-regime over a stored rate, missing credential fatal for
  `meteringcoCalculated` but harmless for everything else.
- Two rules invert a natural instinct. A refused address makes the tax zero and
  the invoice succeed, which reads like swallowing an error. A missing credential
  makes the invoice fail, which reads like the opposite. Both are in the recorded
  month, but only for someone who reads it.
- The stored rate and tax are compared to 1e-9. Rounding to the cent is the most
  natural thing to do to a money field and it fails immediately.
- Nothing announces the exact wire format. The request body, the field names, the
  `shipping: 0`, the joined street on filing and the `provider: "meteringco"` all have
  to be recovered from `tax-authority-log.json`.
- The VAT rule is conditional on country, not on holding a registration, and both
  a US seller and a US buyer hold one specifically to punish the shortcut — on
  each side independently, so getting it right for the buyer is not enough.
- Every recorded row is now the sole instance of its class. There is no second
  example to check a hypothesis against before committing to it.

What makes it tractable, and why I would not estimate lower:

- The prompt names the shape of the capability honestly, and the recorded month
  still covers every rule with a concrete worked example (`DISCOVERABILITY.md`
  maps them one to one).
- The removal is coherent and the seams are obvious: `TaxModule` is gone from
  `app.module.ts`, `tax/` is an empty directory, and `salesTaxRate` is still on
  the entity, in the Influx tags, and in the read DTO, all wired to a constant
  zero. A solver knows where to work within minutes.
- 559 lines is a morning's work to write once the rules are known. The difficulty
  is entirely in the rules, not the volume — which is the honest consequence of
  the capability being smaller than the 1,000-line target.
- Three rules are reachable without the recording at all: the regime names and
  their empty-string third member come from a surviving enum, the exemption from
  another, and the sandbox/production selection from a surviving sibling that
  makes the same choice for Stripe tokens. A solver who reads only the code still
  gets a third of the way.
